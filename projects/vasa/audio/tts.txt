Stockholm. August tenth, sixteen twenty-eight. The Vasa!
Two full gun decks, when most warships had just one.
But before launch, thirty sailors ran side to side across her deck.
A stability test. She rocked so hard, they stopped it.
She sailed anyway.
Less than a mile out, a gust hits her sails. She leans.
Water pours through the open gun ports!
Within minutes, Sweden's mightiest ship is on the seabed.
Then, in nineteen sixty-one, she comes back up. Almost whole!
~How?
You see, warm salty seas are full of shipworms. Tiny clams that chew through wood.
In the Mediterranean, a wreck like this would be eaten within thirty years.
But the Baltic is cold and barely salty, and shipworms can't thrive there.
So for three hundred thirty-three years, the Vasa sat on the floor of Stockholm harbor. Untouched.
Today she is about ninety-eight percent original.
If she had sailed off to war, she would have rotted, burned, or sunk somewhere salty, and vanished.
Instead, over a million people visit her every year!
The worst launch in naval history gave her a perfect hiding place.
Sinking is the only reason this warship still exists.
