Sinking is the only reason this warship still exists.
Stockholm. August 10th, 1628. The Vasa. Two full gun decks, when most warships had just one.
But before launch, thirty sailors ran side to side across her deck. A stability test. She rocked so hard, they stopped it. She sailed anyway.
Less than a mile out, a gust hits her sails. She leans. Water pours through the open gun ports. Within minutes, Sweden's mightiest ship is on the seabed.
Then, in 1961, she comes back up. Almost whole.
How?
You see, warm salty seas are full of shipworms. Tiny clams that chew through wood. In the Mediterranean, a wreck like this would be eaten within thirty years. But the Baltic is cold and barely salty, and shipworms can't thrive there. So for 333 years, the Vasa sat on the floor of Stockholm harbor. Untouched. Today she is about 98 percent original.
If she had sailed off to war, she would have rotted, burned, or sunk somewhere salty, and vanished.
Instead, over a million people visit her every year.
The worst launch in naval history gave her a perfect hiding place. Sinking is the only reason this warship still exists.
