One wrong number turned a brand-new jet into a glider.
July twenty-third, nineteen eighty-three. Air Canada Flight one forty-three.
Sixty-one passengers, eight crew, cruising at forty-one thousand feet over Ontario.
One engine quits. Then the other.
Total silence.
The fuel gauges? Broken since before takeoff.
Nobody knew the tanks were almost empty.
How does a jet just... run dry?
You see, Canada was switching to metric, and the Boeing seven sixty-seven was its first metric plane.
But the fuel was worked out with the old number: one point seven seven pounds per liter, instead of zero point eight kilograms.
So they took off with less than half the fuel they needed.
But luck was on board. The captain flew gliders for fun.
And the co-pilot once served at an old air base nearby. Gimli.
One problem. That runway was now a racetrack.
And today was Family Day. Go-karts everywhere.
The plane drops in silently. The nose slams down. Fire bursts out.
And everyone lives.
That same jet kept flying until two thousand eight.
All because a crew trusted one wrong number.
