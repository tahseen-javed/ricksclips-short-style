One wrong number turned a brand-new jet into a glider.
July 23rd, 1983. Air Canada Flight 143. Sixty-one passengers, eight crew, cruising at 41,000 feet over Ontario.
One engine quits. Then the other. Total silence.
The fuel gauges? Broken since before takeoff. Nobody knew the tanks were almost empty.
How does a jet just... run dry?
You see, Canada was switching to metric, and the Boeing 767 was its first metric plane. But the fuel was worked out with the old number: 1.77 pounds per liter, instead of 0.8 kilograms.
So they took off with less than half the fuel they needed.
But luck was on board. The captain flew gliders for fun. And the co-pilot once served at an old air base nearby. Gimli.
One problem. That runway was now a racetrack. And today was Family Day. Go-karts everywhere.
The plane drops in silently. The nose slams down. Fire bursts out.
And everyone lives.
That same jet kept flying until 2008.
All because a crew trusted one wrong number.
