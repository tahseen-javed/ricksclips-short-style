The Statue of Liberty was never supposed to be green! That famous color is damage!
New York Harbor! October twenty-eighth, eighteen eighty-six! Lady Liberty is unveiled, and she shines like a brand-new penny! Reddish-brown copper, gleaming in the sun! Three hundred hammered sheets, each about as thick as two pennies! But salt air and rain get to work! The shine fades to a dull brown! Then green streaks creep in!
By nineteen-ten, she's half copper, half blue-green! Patchy, blotchy, and spreading! Officials fear the green is eating her alive! Even her designer, Frédéric Bar-tol-dee, guessed wrong! He expected her to darken to red! So in nineteen-oh-six, Congress sets aside sixty-two thousand dollars to paint her back to copper! And the public explodes!
Newspapers call it sacrilege! The plan quietly dies! You see, that green isn't rot! It's a patina! Copper reacts with the air and grows its own thin skin! And that skin seals the metal underneath, shielding it from more corrosion! The damage is the armor!
About ten years later, she's green from crown to toe! She's stayed that way for over a century! So the paint job would have covered the one thing keeping her alive! America nearly erased its most famous color, because the Statue of Liberty was never supposed to be green!
