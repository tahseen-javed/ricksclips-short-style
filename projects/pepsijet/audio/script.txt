Pepsi put a fighter jet in an ad as a joke. A college student tried to buy it.
1996. Pepsi Stuff. Drink Pepsi, collect points, trade them for T-shirts and leather jackets. Then the final shot. A teenager lands a Harrier jump jet at his high school. Price: seven million Pepsi Points.
Nobody can drink that much Pepsi. But John Leonard, a business student, found the loophole. You could buy extra points. Ten cents each. Seven million points? Seven hundred thousand dollars. For a military jet worth over thirty million.
So he found five investors. He mailed Pepsi fifteen labels and a check for seven hundred thousand dollars and eight dollars fifty. Some of that was shipping and handling.
Pepsi sent the check back. He sued.
You see, Pepsi said it was obviously a joke, and in 1999 a federal judge agreed. Judge Kimba Wood called flying a jet to school an exaggerated adolescent fantasy. No objective person, the court said, could see a real offer. Leonard lost. The appeal failed too.
But Pepsi still changed the ad. The jet now cost seven hundred million points. And two new words appeared under the price: just kidding.
Leonard never got his jet. He got a Netflix series instead, in 2022. Because nobody told a college student that Pepsi's fighter jet was a joke.
