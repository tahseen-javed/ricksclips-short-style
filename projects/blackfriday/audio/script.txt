Black Friday wasn't named by happy shopkeepers. It was named by furious cops.

Philadelphia, 1961. The day after Thanksgiving, suburban shoppers flood downtown. Then on Saturday comes the Army-Navy football game, and the tourists pour in too. Gridlock. Crowds. Chaos.

No traffic cop gets the day off. They work twelve-hour shifts. So the police give that Friday a dark name. Black Friday. Stores hate it. That same year, merchants try to rebrand it "Big Friday." Nobody says Big Friday.

You see, the name refuses to die. Slowly, it spreads across the whole country. So retailers switch tactics. If you can't kill a name, change what it means.

In the 1980s, a brand-new story appears. Stores lose money all year, running in the red, and this is the day they finally go into the black. Sounds perfect. It just isn't true.

Today, stores print the cops' insult on their own sale signs. And not one of them will tell you why...
