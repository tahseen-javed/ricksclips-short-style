Mount Rushmore's name was a joke. A New York lawyer asked one question, and a mountain got his name.
1885. The Black Hills of South Dakota. Charles Rushmore is checking mining claims. He points at a granite peak and asks his guide what it's called. The answer, reportedly: it never had a name. We'll call it Rushmore. And it stuck.
Almost forty years later, a state historian named Doane Robinson wants tourists. His first idea? Carve Western legends into the rock. Red Cloud. Lewis and Clark. Buffalo Bill.
You see, the sculptor went bigger. Four presidents, carved down to the waist. More than four hundred workers. Fourteen years. Just under one million dollars. Faces sixty feet tall. And remarkably, nobody died. Then in 1941 the sculptor died, the money ran thin, and the work stopped at the heads.
The lawyer even gave five thousand dollars toward the carving. So the presidents never got their bodies. But the mountain kept its name, all because one New York lawyer asked one simple question.
