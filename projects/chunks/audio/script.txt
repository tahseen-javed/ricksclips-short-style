Five dollars, split two ways, became a 326 million dollar ice cream company.
Ben Cohen and Jerry Greenfield are childhood friends. They decide to make ice cream. Their training? A five-dollar correspondence course from Penn State. They split the cost.
Then they scrape together twelve thousand dollars. Four thousand each, four thousand from a bank.
May 5th, 1978. Burlington, Vermont. Their first shop opens inside a run-down gas station.
Twenty-two years later, Unilever buys the company for 326 million dollars.
But the strangest part isn't the money. It's the chunks.
You see, Ben Cohen has anosmia. He has no sense of smell, and very little sense of taste. Regular ice cream flavors blur together for him. So he judged ice cream by the one thing he could always feel. Texture. Big, heavy chunks, packed in so every spoonful has something to bite. Most companies sold flavor. Ben was selling the one thing he could actually feel.
In his words: ice cream, to me, is about texture.
So America's most famous chunky ice cream was shaped by a man who can barely taste it. And it all started with five dollars, split two ways.
