Britain's best night pilot couldn't see in the dark. A machine could, and a carrot took the credit.

It's 1940. The Blitz. German bombers hit Britain every night. One pilot keeps finding them anyway. John Cunningham. Twenty kills, nineteen in pitch black.

The papers call him Cat's Eyes. His secret? Carrots. Lots of carrots.

You see, the real secret was a radar hidden inside his plane. Britain could not let the enemy learn about it. So the carrot story went everywhere. Posters. A mascot called Doctor Carrot. Carrot fudge. By 1942, Britain had a hundred thousand tons of extra carrots.

But carrots don't give healthy eyes night vision. And historians say the Germans probably never fell for it.

The only people the lie really fooled were us. More than eighty years later, we still believe it. Because Britain's best night pilot couldn't see in the dark.
