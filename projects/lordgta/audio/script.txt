A British lord tried to stop Grand Theft Auto. That was exactly the plan.

May 20th, 1997. The game isn't even in shops yet. But in the House of Lords, Lord Campbell of Croy is already asking the government what it will do about it.

Newspapers howl. Family groups rage.

And who made this monster? A small studio in Dundee, Scotland. The same people who made Lemmings.

Its own publisher had nearly cancelled it.

You see, that publisher, BMG, then hired Max Clifford, a famous British publicist. Co-creator Mike Dailly said Clifford "designed all the outcry." He told the team who he would target, and what they would say. Founder David Jones said every word came true.

The scandal was the advert.

Twenty-nine years later, GTA 6 takes players back to Vice City, Rockstar's version of Miami.

And it all began because a British lord tried to stop Grand Theft Auto.
