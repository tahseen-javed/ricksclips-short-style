Monopoly was invented to make people hate monopolies. It did the exact opposite.

Lizzie Magie is a typist with a grudge. Landlords get rich just by owning land, and their tenants pay for it.

So she designs a board game to prove it. She patents it in 1904. The Landlord's Game.

Here's the strange part. It ships with two rule sets. In one, every player gets richer whenever anyone earns. In the other, you crush your friends with rent until they go broke.

Guess which one people loved.

You see, Lizzie meant the cruel rules as a warning. Play them, feel how unfair they are, then switch to the fair ones. But players skipped the lesson and kept the cruelty. For years, fans copied the board by hand. Players swapped in their own local street names.

Then a man named Charles Darrow learned the game from friends, called it his own invention, and sold it to Parker Brothers in 1935.

And Lizzie? Parker Brothers bought her patent for 500 dollars. Her name disappeared.

Decades later, an economics professor fighting Parker Brothers in court dug up her old patents. The truth came back.

So the next time a friend bankrupts you on Boardwalk, that sting is the whole point. Because Monopoly was invented to make people hate monopolies.
