NASA's math was perfect. That's exactly how they lost a 125 million dollar spacecraft at Mars.

December 11, 1998. Mars Climate Orbiter launches on a nine-month trip. Every engine burn is calculated to the decimal. But the navigators keep seeing its path drift lower. They raise concerns. Nobody fixes it.

September 23, 1999. The orbiter should pass high above the Martian air. Instead, it drops to under 40 miles. Contact is lost. It never calls home again.

You see, the numbers were never wrong. The units were. Lockheed Martin's software reported thrust in pounds. NASA's software read it as newtons. Every push was off by a factor of 4.45.

One missing label, unnoticed for nine months. So when someone tells you their math is perfect, ask one question. Perfect in what units? Because NASA's math was perfect.
